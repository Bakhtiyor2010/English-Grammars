Modal Verbs

CAN

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + can + base verb
Negative Subject + cannot (can't) + base verb
Question Can + subject + base verb?

Ability

  • I can swim well enough to compete in local competitions.
  • She can code in JavaScript and build interactive websites.
  • He can drive long distances without getting tired.
  • We can solve this problem if we work together.
  • They can speak English fluently in professional situations.

Permission (Informal)

  • Can I borrow your pen for a few minutes? I forgot mine.
  • You can leave early if you finish your work on time.
  • Can we start now, or should we wait for the others?
  • You can use my laptop as long as you’re careful with it.
  • Students can ask questions whenever they don’t understand something.

Possibility

  • This road can be dangerous during the winter because of ice.
  • It can get very cold here at night, especially in January.
  • Mistakes can happen when people are under pressure.
  • That can be true in some situations, but not always.
  • He can be very rude sometimes when he’s stressed.

BE ABLE TO

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + am/is/are/was/were + able to + base verb
Negative Subject + am/is/are not able to + base verb
Question Am/Is/Are + subject + able to + base verb?

Ability (All Tenses)

  • I am able to solve this problem if I concentrate.
  • She was able to finish the project on time despite difficulties.
  • We will be able to attend the meeting next week.
  • He is able to swim across the river without stopping.
  • They were able to escape from the dangerous situation safely.

COULD

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + could + base verb
Negative Subject + could not (couldn't) + base verb
Question Could + subject + base verb?

Past Ability

  • I could swim across the lake when I was six, even though it was cold.
  • She could speak English fluently after living abroad for two years.
  • We could see the ocean clearly from the top of the hill.
  • He could run very fast, faster than anyone in his class.
  • They could solve complex problems if they worked together.

Polite Request

  • Could you help me with this exercise, please?
  • Could I use your phone for a moment? Mine isn’t working.
  • Could you repeat that more slowly? I didn’t understand.
  • Could we leave early if the work is finished?
  • Could you close the door quietly? The baby is sleeping.

Possibility

  • It could rain later, so don’t forget your umbrella.
  • This plan could fail if we don’t prepare properly.
  • She could be at home, or maybe she went out for shopping.
  • That could be the answer, but we need to check again.
  • He could win the match if he practices hard.

MUST

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + must + base verb
Negative Subject + must not (mustn't) + base verb
Question Must + subject + base verb?

Strong Obligation

  • You must wear a seatbelt whenever you drive for safety.
  • Students must follow the rules to avoid getting into trouble.
  • We must finish this work today if we want to meet the deadline.
  • He must apologize to her for what happened yesterday.
  • You must study harder if you want to pass the exam.

Strong Probability

  • She must be tired after working all day without a break.
  • They must be at home because their car is in the driveway.
  • This must be the answer, since it matches all the clues.
  • He must be joking; nobody could really say that seriously.
  • You must be hungry after running for so long.

HAVE TO

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + have/has to + base verb
Negative Subject + do/does not have to + base verb
Question Do/Does + subject + have to + base verb?

Obligation (External Rule)

  • I have to wake up early tomorrow because I have an important meeting.
  • She has to wear a uniform at school every day.
  • We have to follow the rules to avoid getting into trouble.
  • He has to finish the project before the deadline next week.
  • They have to pay taxes by the end of the month.

Necessity

  • I have to call her to explain what happened.
  • She has to improve her skills if she wants a promotion.
  • We have to make a decision before the meeting starts.
  • He has to study tonight for tomorrow’s exam.
  • They have to leave now if they want to catch the train.

No Obligation (Negative)

  • You don’t have to come if you are busy.
  • She doesn’t have to pay because it is free for students.
  • We don’t have to hurry; the store is open until 10 PM.
  • He doesn’t have to work today because it’s a holiday.
  • They don’t have to wait if the bus is late.

MAY

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + may + base verb
Negative Subject + may not + base verb
Question May + subject + base verb?

Formal Permission

  • May I come in? I have something important to discuss.
  • You may begin the presentation when you are ready.
  • May we sit here if the other seats are full?
  • You may leave early if your work is finished.
  • May I ask a question about the assignment?

Possibility

  • It may rain tonight, so don’t forget your umbrella.
  • She may be late because of traffic.
  • He may not agree with our plan, but we can try to convince him.
  • They may visit us during the holidays if they have time.
  • The results may change after the review.

MIGHT

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + might + base verb
Negative Subject + might not + base verb
Question Might + subject + base verb?

Weak Possibility

  • It might snow tomorrow, so you should wear warm clothes.
  • She might join us if she finishes her homework on time.
  • He might forget to bring the book, so remind him.
  • We might be late because of the traffic jam.
  • They might cancel the meeting if the manager is unavailable.

Polite Suggestion

  • You might try restarting it to fix the problem.
  • She might consider studying more before the exam.
  • We might take a different route to avoid traffic.
  • You might ask the teacher for clarification if you are unsure.
  • He might want to check again to make sure it’s correct.

SHOULD

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + should + base verb
Negative Subject + should not (shouldn't) + base verb
Question Should + subject + base verb?

Advice

  • You should study more if you want to pass the exam.
  • She should rest after working all day.
  • He should apologize to avoid conflict.
  • We should leave now to catch the last bus.
  • They should practice daily to improve their skills.

Expectation

  • The train should arrive soon, according to the schedule.
  • She should be home by now; it’s already 8 PM.
  • He should finish the project today if he works hard.
  • The results should improve after the next update.
  • It should stop raining soon, according to the weather forecast.

Probability

  • This should work if we follow the instructions carefully.
  • That should be enough food for everyone at the party.
  • Everything should be fine if we double-check the details.
  • The package should arrive tomorrow if the delivery is on time.
  • He should win easily if he plays like he did last time.

OUGHT TO

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + ought to + base verb
Negative Subject + ought not to + base verb
Question Ought + subject + to + base verb?

Moral Obligation / Advice

  • You ought to apologize to her for what happened yesterday.
  • We ought to respect others, even if we disagree with them.
  • He ought to study more if he wants to pass the exam.
  • They ought to help their classmates when they are in trouble.
  • You ought not to lie, because it can break trust.

SHALL

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + shall + base verb
Negative Subject + shall not (shan't) + base verb
Question Shall + subject + base verb?

Suggestion (with I/We)

  • Shall we begin the meeting now, or wait for everyone?
  • Shall I open the window to let some fresh air in?
  • Shall we go for a walk after lunch?
  • Shall I help you with your homework?
  • Shall we order food online if we are too busy to cook?

Formal Future

  • I shall return before dinner.
  • We shall overcome these difficulties if we work together.
  • The meeting shall begin at 9 sharp, according to the schedule.
  • Payment shall be made tomorrow as agreed in the contract.
  • The tenant shall pay rent monthly on the first day of each month.

WILL

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + will + base verb
Negative Subject + will not (won't) + base verb
Question Will + subject + base verb?

Future

  • I will call you as soon as I finish work.
  • She will arrive tomorrow, so we should be ready to meet her.
  • They will win if they play like they did last time.
  • We will finish soon if everyone helps with the work.
  • He will study abroad next year to improve his skills.

Promise / Decision

  • I will help you with your project, don’t worry.
  • I will not forget your birthday this year.
  • We will support you in your new job.
  • I will do it now, before it gets too late.
  • I will try harder to improve my English.

WOULD

Form

Type Structure
Statement Subject + would + base verb
Negative Subject + would not (wouldn't) + base verb
Question Would + subject + base verb?

Polite Request

  • Would you help me with this exercise, please?
  • Would you open the door for me? It’s very heavy.
  • Would you like tea or coffee while you wait?
  • Would you explain this part again? I didn’t understand it.
  • Would you wait a moment while I finish this call?

Past Habit

  • When I was young, I would play outside every afternoon with my friends.
  • She would visit us often during the holidays.
  • He would read a book every night before going to sleep.
  • We would travel to the countryside every summer.
  • They would walk to school together every morning.
